Aged Care FAQs - Questions and Answers
Section Title
Understanding Aged Care in Australia

FAQ 1
Question: What is a Refundable Accommodation Deposit (RAD)?
Answer: A RAD is a lump sum payment you make to an aged care facility for your accommodation. It's fully refundable when you leave the facility, minus any retention amounts (up to 2% per year, capped at 10% total). The facility holds this money interest-free and must refund it to you or your estate within 14 days of you permanently leaving. You can choose to pay all RAD, all Daily Accommodation Payment (DAP), or a combination of both.

FAQ 2
Question: What is the difference between a RAD and a DAP?
Answer: A RAD is a lump sum payment that you get back when you leave care. A DAP is a daily rental payment calculated using the Maximum Permissible Interest Rate (MPIR) set by the government. For example, if a facility's accommodation price is $400,000, you can pay $400,000 as a RAD (refundable), or pay approximately $90-110 per day as a DAP (not refundable), or split it - perhaps $200,000 RAD plus $45-55 per day DAP. The total accommodation cost is the same; you're just choosing how to pay it.

FAQ 3
Question: How much does aged care really cost in Australia?
Answer: Costs vary widely based on your income, assets, and the facility. Everyone pays a basic daily fee (85% of the single Age Pension, currently around $60/day or $22,000/year). Accommodation costs (RAD/DAP) range from $0 to $550,000+ depending on the facility. If you have assets or income above certain thresholds, you may also pay a means-tested care fee (up to $33,000/year capped). Total annual costs can range from $22,000 to $80,000+ per year depending on your financial situation. The Aged Care Decision Guide breaks down all these costs with real examples and calculators.

FAQ 4
Question: Do I need an ACAT assessment to enter aged care?
Answer: Yes, you must have an Aged Care Assessment Team (ACAT) assessment before entering permanent residential aged care in Australia. ACAT assesses your care needs and approves your eligibility for government-funded aged care. You can request an assessment through My Aged Care (1800 200 422) or your GP can refer you. The assessment is free and usually takes 2-4 weeks, though urgent cases can be prioritized.

FAQ 5
Question: What is means testing for aged care?
Answer: Means testing determines how much you contribute to your care costs based on your income and assets. Services Australia (Centrelink) conducts the assessment, looking at your Age Pension, other income, savings, investments, and property. Depending on your total assets and income, you may pay a means-tested care fee on top of basic fees. Your family home may be exempt or partially exempt if your spouse still lives there or under certain other circumstances. The assessment affects both your accommodation costs and care fees.

FAQ 6
Question: How do I choose the right aged care facility?
Answer: Visit at least 3-5 facilities in person, ideally during meal times. Check their star ratings on My Aged Care website (government quality ratings). Ask about staff-to-resident ratios, activities programs, and specialized care (dementia, palliative, etc.). Review the accommodation agreement carefully - understand all costs including RAD/DAP, extra service fees, and what happens if you run out of money. Check proximity to family and friends for regular visits. The Aged Care Decision Guide includes a comprehensive 25-point facility evaluation checklist.

FAQ 7
Question: Can I protect my family home from aged care costs?
Answer: Your home may be exempt from the assets test if your spouse or dependent child still lives there. If you're single and your home is valued under the capped amount (currently $204,000), it's fully exempt. Above that amount, only $204,000 is exempt. You can rent out your home and the rental income is assessed. You're not forced to sell your home to pay aged care costs, but you'll need to pay accommodation costs another way (through a RAD, DAP, or combination). Many families use the rental income to cover DAP costs. Seek advice from an aged care financial adviser for your specific situation.

FAQ 8
Question: What happens to my RAD when I die?
Answer: Your RAD (minus any retention amounts, unpaid fees, or additional charges) is refundable to your estate. The facility must refund the balance within 14 days of receiving all required documentation (including probate if needed). If you paid a combination RAD/DAP, the RAD portion is refundable. Any retention amounts (up to 2% per year, capped at 10% total) are kept by the facility. Make sure your executor knows about the RAD and has copies of your accommodation agreement.

FAQ 9
Question: How long does it take to get into aged care?
Answer: After receiving ACAT approval, timing varies significantly. Some facilities have immediate vacancies and you could move in within days (especially for emergency placements). Popular facilities in desirable locations may have waiting lists of several months. On average, expect 2-8 weeks from ACAT approval to moving in if you're not in crisis. For emergency placements (hospital discharge, unsafe at home), placements can happen within 24-48 hours, though you may need to accept whatever is available initially and transfer later.

FAQ 10
Question: What's the difference between high care and low care?
Answer: Australia no longer officially uses "high care" and "low care" classifications - this changed in 2014. Now all residential aged care is simply "residential aged care" and facilities must provide the level of care you need as assessed by ACAT. However, you'll still hear these terms used informally. What matters now is your ACAT assessment which determines your care needs and the government funding the facility receives to care for you. Some facilities specialize in higher-dependency residents (dementia, palliative care), while others focus on more independent residents.

FAQ 11
Question: Can my partner and I stay together in aged care?
Answer: Yes, many facilities offer couple's accommodation. You can share a room if both of you require residential care and the facility has couple's rooms available. If only one of you needs care, the other can sometimes stay as a "social" resident (paying full costs without government subsidies). Costs are calculated individually based on each person's means test. Not all facilities offer couple's accommodation, so ask specifically when touring facilities if this is important to you.

FAQ 12
Question: What if I run out of money to pay for aged care?
Answer: If you've used all your assets to pay aged care costs (including any RAD), you become a "supported resident." The government then pays your accommodation costs and you pay a basic daily fee only (currently around $60/day). Facilities cannot evict you for inability to pay if you've genuinely used your assets for care. However, you must disclose your financial situation honestly - hiding assets can result in penalties. This is one reason many people choose partial RAD/partial DAP strategies to preserve assets longer.

FAQ 13
Question: Is aged care at home cheaper than residential care?
Answer: Home Care Packages (HCP) can be cheaper initially but may become more expensive as care needs increase. HCP levels 1-4 provide $9,000 to $58,000 per year in government funding, but you still pay for additional services, home modifications, and living costs. Residential care provides 24/7 care, meals, accommodation, and activities in one package. For low-level care needs, home care is usually cheaper. For high care needs (dementia, mobility issues, 24-hour supervision), residential care often becomes more cost-effective. The decision depends on care needs, home suitability, and personal preference, not just cost.

FAQ 14
Question: What should I bring to aged care?
Answer: Bring personal items that make your room feel like home: photos, small furniture if space allows, favorite chair, blankets, TV (check facility rules). Clothing should be comfortable, easy to wash, and labeled with your name. Bring medications in original packaging and a current medication list. Most facilities provide bed, bedding, meals, and basic toiletries. Don't bring large amounts of cash or valuable jewelry. Check your facility's specific list - most provide a "what to bring" guide. The Aged Care Decision Guide includes a complete packing checklist.

FAQ 15
Question: Can I change facilities after moving in?
Answer: Yes, you can move to a different facility, though there are financial implications. Your RAD from the first facility is refundable (minus retention and fees) and can be used toward the new facility's RAD. You'll need a new accommodation agreement with the new facility. Some people do temporary placements while waiting for their preferred facility to have availability. Give proper notice to your current facility (check your agreement - usually 14 days). You don't need a new ACAT assessment to transfer between facilities.
