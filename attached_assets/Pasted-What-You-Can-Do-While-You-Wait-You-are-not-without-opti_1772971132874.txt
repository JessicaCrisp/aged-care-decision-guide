What You Can Do While You Wait
You are not without options during this period. Here is what families in this situation should know.
Use the Commonwealth Home Support Programme (CHSP) as a bridge. This is entry-level government-funded support — things like domestic assistance, personal care, meals, and transport. It's not as comprehensive as a Home Care Package but it can provide meaningful help while you wait. Ask your ACAT assessor specifically about CHSP access.
Consider privately funded interim care. If your family has the financial capacity, privately paying for some support services while waiting for a package can make the difference between managing at home and being forced into a premature residential care decision. It is worth getting quotes from home care providers for privately funded hours.
Keep your contact details updated with My Aged Care. If your circumstances change — if the person's condition deteriorates, if a carer becomes unavailable, if there is a hospitalisation — contact My Aged Care immediately. A change in circumstances can trigger a priority reassessment which may move you up the queue.
Request a reassessment if the situation has changed. The original ACAT assessment reflects the person's condition at the time it was done. If things have gotten significantly worse since then, you may be eligible for a higher priority rating. Don't assume the original assessment still reflects current needs.
Talk to the hospital if there has been a recent admission. Hospital social workers have direct pathways to expedite assessments and interim support for people being discharged. If your parent has recently been in hospital, this is the moment to push hard for a package or bridging support.
The Bigger Picture
The wait for home care is one of the reasons families end up making rushed residential aged care decisions they later regret. Someone is managing at home, a package is taking too long, a health event happens, and suddenly the family is in a hospital corridor being told they need to find a facility by Friday.
Understanding that this situation is foreseeable — and plannable — is the first step. The families who navigate it best are the ones who applied for assessment early, used CHSP support as a bridge, and had a clear picture of what residential care would cost if it became necessary.
That last part matters more than most families realise. Residential aged care fees — means-tested care fees, accommodation costs, daily fees — can vary by tens of thousands of dollars per year depending on decisions made in the first weeks. Knowing what those decisions are before you're forced to make them is exactly what separates families who feel in control from families who feel blindsided.
The Bottom Line
If you're waiting for a Home Care Package right now, take three steps today. Contact My Aged Care to confirm your place in the queue and check your priority rating. Ask about CHSP support as an immediate bridge. And start getting across what residential aged care would actually cost your family — not because you're giving up on keeping your parent at home, but because being informed about both paths is always better than being informed about neither.
