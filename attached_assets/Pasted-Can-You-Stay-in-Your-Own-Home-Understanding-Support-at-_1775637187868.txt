Can You Stay in Your Own Home? Understanding Support at Home for Australian Seniors
Published April 3, 2026 | Updated for 2026 regulations
When Margaret's doctor suggested she might need aged care, her first thought was: "I'm not ready to leave my home." Like most Australians, Margaret wanted to age in the place where she'd raised her family and built her life.
The good news? The Australian Government's Support at Home program (you might know it as Home Care Packages) makes staying home possible for many older Australians. But navigating the system can be confusing. Here's what you need to know.
What is Support at Home?
Think of Support at Home as government funding that comes to you, rather than you going to a facility. The money pays for services delivered to your home - everything from help with showering and medication to home modifications like grab rails or a stair lift.
The program replaced the old four-tier Home Care Package system in 2024, and the new version is more flexible. Your funding level now matches your specific needs rather than forcing you into predetermined categories.
Common services funded include personal care, nursing visits, physiotherapy, cleaning, meal preparation, equipment, and even transport to medical appointments.
How Much Does It Actually Cost?
Here's the part everyone asks about first: what will you pay?
Everyone contributes a basic daily fee - currently around $11.50 per day, or roughly $4,200 a year. If you have higher income or assets, you'll pay an additional income-tested fee, capped at about $13,000 annually.
The government then tops up your funding based on your care needs. Total funding can range from $10,000 for basic support to over $60,000 for complex care needs.
Real example: Joan receives $35,000 in government funding and pays $10,200 herself (basic fee plus income-tested contribution). Her total support package is worth $45,200 per year - enough for daily personal care, weekly nursing visits, fortnightly physiotherapy, and regular cleaning.
How Do You Qualify?
Start by calling My Aged Care on 1800 200 422. You'll have an initial chat about your needs, followed by a comprehensive face-to-face assessment if you're eligible.
The assessor will visit your home, evaluate what help you need, and determine your funding level. They'll also check if your home is safe and suitable, or if modifications are needed.
From first contact to services starting typically takes 4-12 weeks. If you're in an urgent situation - say, being discharged from hospital - the process can be fast-tracked.
The Question Everyone Asks: Home or Facility?
This is deeply personal, but here's a practical way to think about it.
Support at Home works well when you need help with daily tasks but don't require round-the-clock supervision. You can manage overnight on your own (or have family nearby), your home is suitable or can be modified, and you value staying in your community.
Residential care becomes the better option when you need 24-hour supervision, have complex medical needs, live alone without nearby support, or your home isn't safe even with modifications.
The honest truth: Many people eventually transition from home support to residential care as their needs increase. That's normal and okay. Support at Home isn't an all-or-nothing decision - it's often a valuable stage in the aged care journey.
What Most People Don't Realize
The new Support at Home system has one significant advantage over the old packages: the funding follows you. If your provider isn't meeting your needs, you can switch without losing your funding or starting over.
You also have more say in how the money is spent. Want to prioritize physiotherapy over cleaning? You can make that choice with your provider.
Common Mistakes to Avoid
Don't wait until crisis hits. Many people only contact My Aged Care after a fall or hospital stay, then face frustrating delays when they need help immediately. Register early, even if you don't need services yet.
Don't assume you can't afford it. The means testing is complex, and many people with modest assets qualify for substantial government support.
And don't go it alone. The system is complicated, and small decisions about how you structure your finances can make a significant difference to what you pay.
Getting Started
If you're reading this and thinking "this sounds like me" or "this sounds like Mum," make that call to My Aged Care today: 1800 200 422.
Have your Medicare number handy, think about what help you need, and be honest about your situation. The assessors have seen it all, and there's no shame in needing support.
Staying in your own home with the right support isn't just possible - for many Australians, it's the best choice. But like any aged care decision, it works best when you understand your options and plan ahead.
